The Data Moat

Everyone has read speech.
No one has conversation.

2,384+

Zero
Hours of conversational Kinyarwanda or Kiswahili in any public corpus on Earth

The Vision

The voice layer for African daily life.

Africa's informal economy runs on voice, trust, and relationships. The AI economy runs on structured data. Ijwi is the bridge between them.

Daily commerce here doesn't happen in dashboards. It happens in voice notes, WhatsApp chats, and mobile money transfers — in Kinyarwanda, Kiswahili, and 2,000 other languages. The same voice technology that keeps families connected can give that economy a memory.

Speak your business

“I sold 12 crates. Jean paid half. 3 bags left.”

A market seller talks; Ijwi keeps the books. Sales, debts, and stock — recorded in her own language, no forms, no accounting words.

Where people already are

Voice notes. WhatsApp. Mobile money.

No new habits to learn. Ijwi meets commerce in the channels it already lives in — and answers back in the language people actually speak.

Records that open doors

From voice notes to credit-ready.

With explicit consent — and only then — those records can become a business profile a cooperative or lender can trust. The user decides. Always.

$67B
Africa's mobile money market by 2030 — and almost none of it can be operated by voice in the languages its users speak. Family threads today. Economic infrastructure tomorrow.
